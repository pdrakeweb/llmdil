Workshop migration plan — constrained scheduling problem

A two-person workshop is moving its fabrication work from a rented
unit (Unit A) to a larger owned building (Unit B) over a three-week
window. The landlord requires Unit A to be fully empty by the end of
day 21. Unit B cannot accept equipment until its electrical work is
signed off. Produce a day-by-day plan that satisfies every constraint
below, and answer the questions at the end.

People and availability
- Mara (electrician, workshop lead): works days 1–21 except Sundays
  (days 7, 14, 21). Only Mara may do electrical work or sign off a
  machine's reconnection.
- Devon (machinist): works days 1–21 except days 10–12 (booked
  elsewhere). Only Devon may move the CNC mill or recalibrate it.
- Sam (general help): works any day, but can only do tasks marked
  "anyone", and only one task at a time.
- No person does more than one task per day. A task that needs two
  people occupies both of them for its whole duration.

Equipment and tasks (duration in whole days; dependencies must finish
the day before a dependent task starts)

  #  Task                                      Dur.  Who       Depends on
  1  Pack hand tools and benches               2     anyone    —
  2  Disconnect CNC mill                        1     Devon     —
  3  Move CNC mill to Unit B                    1     Devon+Sam 2
  4  Disconnect lathe                           1     anyone    —
  5  Move lathe to Unit B                       1     Sam       4
  6  Unit B: install 3-phase feed               3     Mara      —

Hard constraints
- Floor painting (task 16) must finish before any equipment is placed
  in Unit B (tasks 8 and 11), because placed equipment cannot be
  moved again once levelled. This overrides the dependency column
  where they disagree.
- The CNC mill and lathe may not be moved on the same day (one truck).
- Task 7 (inspection) can only happen on a weekday, and the inspector
  needs Mara present for the whole day.
- At most one "Move" task (3, 5, 13, 15) per day — one truck.
- Production commitment: the lathe must be reconnected and tested
  (task 12 complete) by the end of day 15, because a customer job
  starts on the lathe on day 16.

Soft goals, in priority order
1. Finish everything as early as possible.
2. Subject to that, minimize Mara's idle days in the final week,
   because she is also covering support calls then.
3. Subject to that, prefer moving the dust collector (task 13) before
   the workbenches (task 15), so ducting measurements can be taken
   while Unit B is still mostly empty.

Questions
1. Give the day-by-day schedule: for each day 1–21, list who does
   what, or "idle". Every task above must appear exactly once, with
   its full duration on consecutive days by the same person (or pair).
2. State the day the whole move completes (task 18 done) and show
   that no earlier completion is possible: identify the critical
   chain of tasks and availabilities that forces that date.
3. Verify the production commitment: on which day does task 12
   finish, and how much slack does it have?
4. If Devon loses one further day — day 5 — which tasks move, and
   does the completion date change? If it does not, explain which
   slack absorbed the loss; if it does, give the new date and the
   new critical chain.
5. List every constraint you used that is stated in this prompt
   outside the dependency column, so a checker can confirm none were
   silently dropped.

Format the schedule as a table with one row per day and columns
Day | Mara | Devon | Sam. Keep the reasoning for questions 2–4
tight: name tasks by number and day, do not restate the whole
schedule.
